ROOT CAUSE
  Messages' camera is Apple's CameraMessagesApp extension (Camera.app/PlugIns/
  CameraMessagesApp.appex) shown in a remote view inside Messages. The preview and the
  shutter / flip / flash / close buttons are drawn and handled in the extension's own
  process (not injected, so it never logged anything). A tap reaches them only if the window
  server finds no window of Messages' own drawn over that spot first. The stage keeps Messages'
  keyboard windows (UIRemoteKeyboardWindow / UITextEffectsWindow, with the input and dictation
  pieces) card-sized at the top of the window stack, as the log shows at 15:55:54.256. A
  touch on the camera therefore went to Messages, not to the extension. The stage's keyboard
  hit filters then dropped it to the window below. That works for Messages' own views, but
  Messages never passes a touch on to the extension, so every camera button was dead. The
  preview still showed because drawing is not touch.
  The transform hypothesis (UIKit hit testing in untransformed bounds) is not what this log
  shows: the transform was never applied. UIKit's hit testing also honours transforms. The
  dial-pad problem in 648/649 was keys moved outside their parent's bounds.
  Caveat: the log has no per-tap evidence (4.5.665 had no tap logging), so this is the most
  likely cause, not a proven one. 4.5.666 adds the tap666 lines to settle it.

FIX (app dylib only; SpringBoard unchanged apart from the version)
  app/DSCameraStage.m
    + DSCam666UpdateTouchPath (main thread, on camera screen appear/disappear, keyboard
      show/hide, and every 0.5 s while a camera screen is up):
      camera up = staged, not Phone, and a camera screen (CKFunCameraViewController, image
      picker, any camera-named VC) visible on screen.
      While it is up and no keyboard (>=120pt) is up: every visible UITextEffectsWindow /
      UIRemoteKeyboardWindow / *KeyboardWindow covering >=25% of the card that holds neither
      the camera screen nor a remote view (_UIRemoteView, _UISizeTrackingView,
      *RemoteViewController, *MessageExtension*) is hidden (taken out of the touch path) and
      remembered weakly. Those windows are put back the moment the camera screen goes, a
      keyboard comes up, or the app leaves the card.
    + DSCameraStageCameraScreenUp() (atomic read) and DSCameraStageNoteHit() (tap log).
  app/Tweak.xm
    While a camera screen is up, the stage's keyboard hit filters step aside (plain %orig):
    UIView hitTest/pointInside, UIWindow hitTest, UIAutoRotatingWindow hitTest,
    UITextEffectsWindow hitTest, UIEditingOverlayGestureView pointInside. The Phone dial-pad
    routing (DSPhoneRouteLiftedHit) runs before this and is unchanged.
  Logs:
    tap666 <bundle> camera screen up|gone; windows: <class lvl frame hidden camera>; ...
    tap666 <bundle> camera up: <window> lvl=.. <frame> taken out of the touch path (it sat over the camera)
    tap666 <bundle> put N keyboard window(s) back (<why>)
    tap666 <bundle> point=x,y window=<class> lvl=.. hit=<class> vc=<owner VC> remote=0|1 path=..
      (at most 3 per tap, one per window asked, 60 per minute)
    A tap with no tap666 line went straight to the camera extension (the good case for
    Messages) or to SpringBoard.

RISKS
  - If the extension's touches are blocked by something other than Messages' keyboard
    windows (e.g. the extension refusing touches while its host is hosted by SpringBoard),
    the buttons stay dead. The tap666 lines will show that: taps on the shutter that log
    hit=_UIRemoteView/remote=1 or no line at all.
  - Hiding a keyboard window while the camera is up: if the camera itself needs a text field
    (rare), the keyboard notification puts the windows back first.
  - For in-process cameras (Beeper / Signal / Messenger) the only change is that the keyboard
    filters step aside while their camera screen is up; if their buttons still fail, the
    tap666 lines show the hit view so the next fix can be targeted.
